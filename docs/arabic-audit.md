# Arabic source and rendering audit — 25 September 2026

## Quran

All 6,236 numbered ayahs were compared, without Unicode normalization, against
the Arabic returned by the [QuranEnc API](https://quranenc.com/en/home/api/):
`https://quranenc.com/api/v1/translation/sura/english_mokhtasar/{chapter}`.
The Arabic field is used; the translation field was not imported.

The original corpus matched all 6,236 entries in the declared
[GitHub source](https://github.com/asim/quran-json-arabic), whose download script
attributes its Uthmani text to QuranEnc. However, that snapshot differed from
the current primary source in 1,108 verses. 1,106 differences consist only of
tatweel and spacing. Two require special attention:

- **2:72:** the old representation of `فَٱدَّٰرَٰءۡتُمۡ` contained a thin space
  and a different hamza representation.
- **11:41:** the mark in `مَجۡر۪ىٰهَا` differs (U+065C in the old source,
  U+06EA in the current source).

`quran/data/arabic.json` now matches all 6,236 current source verses exactly.
SHA-256: `1bf198a7ff2e948cf8c882755adb4b5142c06f12cb8f9e8ef0762c068997c195`.
No Arabic was generated by an LLM or corrected from memory.

Reproduce the comparison with `python scripts/verify_arabic.py`.
`go test ./quran` also verifies every loaded verse, every word-view
reconstruction, numbered lookup, counts, and the audited corpus checksum.

The Bismillah is an unnumbered opening for chapters other than 1 and 9.
It is not included in `verse_count`, and cannot displace numbered API/MCP
lookups. Al-Fatiha 1:1 is itself the Bismillah.

## Word-by-word and rendering

Learning annotations originate from [quranwbw](https://github.com/asim/quranwbw).
A previous conversion replaced Arabic words where counts matched, leaving
eight verses in another script. The loader now aligns annotations to the
canonical text. Where one source groups two words together, their annotation
is grouped rather than assigning it to the wrong word. Concatenating the
Arabic annotation groups reproduces the complete verse exactly, including
recitation and pause marks. English glosses and transliterations are learning
aids, not independently certified translations or pronunciation guides.

Chapter and single-verse views use the same Arabic component. Normal reading
renders the full string, while learning mode uses right-to-left groups and
separate left-to-right annotations. Verse zero has no numbered ayah marker.
Arabic verse anchors exist in every reading mode. JSON, Markdown, lite HTML,
embeds and transcripts receive their Quran Arabic from the same loaded corpus.

Both bundled font copies are identical **Noto Naskh Arabic** fonts, distributed
under the included SIL Open Font License, from the [Google Fonts source](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl/notonaskharabic).
The cmap covers all non-whitespace code points in the refreshed corpus.
Browser checks of 11:41, 2:72 and 2:255 confirmed rendering of the corrected
marks; the previous font displayed a dotted circle for U+06EA in 11:41.
Glyph coverage is not a substitute for scholarly proofreading or checking
every browser's shaping. Existing exported copies are not retroactively updated.

## Names, hadith and commentary: limits of verification

- All 99 Arabic names match the declared
  [99-Names-Of-Allah source](https://github.com/asim/99-Names-Of-Allah) exactly.
  This verifies transcription against that source, not scholarly authentication
  of every accompanying description or reference.
- The Bukhari import contains 14,554 raw records, all with Arabic, and is
  deduplicated on load. The scraper names **Sunnah.com** as its source and stores
  the matn separately from the sanad. It has no pinned source snapshot or
  per-record source hashes. A complete live, record-by-record Arabic comparison
  has **not** been established. Do not claim the whole hadith corpus is verified.
- The stored `tafsir.json` has no author, edition, source URL or licence metadata
  in its import commit. Its attribution remains **unverified**. The UI calls it
  stored commentary; do not attribute it to a named scholar without evidence.
- Explain receives the exact selected verse, its translation and stored
  commentary. Its output is labelled AI explanation, cannot replace canonical
  Arabic, and is instructed not to invent circumstances, rulings or attribution.
  Without a model, it displays stored commentary. This does not authenticate the
  commentary or make the generated explanation authoritative.

The Quran source comparison is complete. A blanket claim that every Arabic
resource in the app has been independently authenticated would be inaccurate.
